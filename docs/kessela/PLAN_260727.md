## 8. "We need to make the studies"

The 8 articles now live at `/posts` are **their WordPress copy, verbatim**. They
carry their claims, they read dated, and at least one is a claim in the title:
*"What advanced science studies from Harvard, Stanford & published medical
journals say…"*. **If Harvard and Stanford are not actually in the citations, that
title has to go** — asserting an institution's endorsement is a bigger exposure
than any sentence in the body.

### The format that makes this a moat instead of a liability

One article per real published paper, structured the same way every time:

1. **The citation** — authors, journal, year, link. Verifiable.
2. **What they did** — participants, duration, what was measured, how.
3. **What they found** — the actual reported result, with the actual numbers.
4. **What this does NOT show** — the honest limits. Sample size, no control arm,
   circumference measured rather than fat mass, funded by a manufacturer.
5. **What it means if you're considering this** — carefully, inside §6.2's list.

**Section 4 is the whole strategy.** Every competitor in this category overstates,
so a page that volunteers *"this study had 40 participants and measured
circumference, not fat mass"* reads as the only honest source in the industry.
That is what makes someone trust a $599 purchase from a brand they've never heard
of. It is also, conveniently, the version that survives contact with a regulator,
a journalist, or a Meta reviewer.

### What it needs

- **The papers themselves.** Some are named in the imported titles; each needs to
  be found, read, and checked that it says what the title claims.
- **A decision per article**: keep and rewrite, or retire. Some of the 8 may not
  survive step 1, and retiring one is a better outcome than dressing it up.
- **The incontinence literature**, which nobody has looked at yet and which is
  probably the strongest evidence base of the lot — pelvic-floor stimulation has
  far better trial support than body contouring does.

### Why it earns

Two jobs from one piece of content: **organic acquisition** (a study article is
the search result someone lands on months before they buy) and **closing** (the
person who has the price in a tab and needs a reason to trust it). Nothing else
on the site does both.

**Blocked on §6.2** for what the "what it means for you" section is allowed to
say. Everything else — finding, reading, summarising, the honest-limits section —
can start immediately and is the bulk of the work.

## 13. Getting paid, and the ad accounts

### 13.1 The 10% IS the platform fee

Ken is the platform, so the two are one pocket. That makes the plumbing simple
and it makes it automatic: Stripe's `application_fee_amount` deducts the cut at
checkout, so the money arrives without an invoice, a reminder, or a conversation
at unit 900. That is a much better position than billing monthly.

**Two blockers, in order:**

1. **Kessela has no connected Stripe account.** `stripeAccountId: null`,
   onboarding never completed — so today every dollar taken on the site lands in
   Ken's Stripe rather than David's. The checkout works; the money just goes to
   the wrong account. **This is item #1 at the setup meeting**, above the server:
   David completes Stripe Connect, his name goes on the receipt, and the fee
   split becomes possible at all.
2. **The platform fee is node-wide.** It is 500 bps (5%) for the whole node by
   design — `platformFee.ts` says per-tenant is "a deliberate future decision,
   not something to fall into by accident." Setting it to 1000 bps to collect
   Ken's 10% from Kessela would also charge Clearwater and every other tenant
   10%. So this needs a **per-tenant override** built first. Small slice, and it
   is the difference between getting paid automatically and getting paid when
   someone remembers.

**Until both are done, the 10% is a handshake, not a mechanism.** Worth saying
plainly because everything else is built and this is the part that pays.

### 13.2 No bank draft — David is right

Bank draft/ACH kills an impulse purchase. The buyer for this is image-conscious
and comfortable at $599; that person taps a card and does not go and find a
routing number. ACH is for recurring billing and for rent, not for this.

**"No ACH" is not "card only"** — see §13.6. Card plus *financing* is the rail,
and financing is what makes $599 a monthly decision instead of a single one. The
two are the same argument: remove friction at the moment of deciding.

### 13.3 The ad accounts — what actually has to happen

**Correction worth having up front: Google *Ads* is not the Google API key.**
They are separate accounts with separate billing. The API key does maps, mail,
calendar and Gemini. Ads is its own product, its own verification, its own card.
Meta is separate again. Nothing chains off the API key.

| # | Account | Time | What actually gates it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Meta Business Suite** + ad account | ~1 hr | **Business verification can take days** — start it first even if nothing runs for a fortnight |
| 2 | **Domain verification** (Meta) | ~15 min | A DNS TXT record. Required before iOS-era conversion tracking works at all |
| 3 | **Meta Pixel / Conversions API** | ~1 hr | Must fire on purchase, and be marked as a *health-adjacent* business |
| 4 | **Google Ads** | ~1 hr | Medical-device advertising is a **restricted category** — expect a review, and expect some ad copy to be rejected |
| 5 | **Google Merchant Center** | ~1 hr | Only if we want Shopping listings. Needs the product feed and a returns policy on the site — both now exist |
| 6 | **Google Analytics / Tag** | ~30 min | Optional: first-touch attribution already runs on the platform |

Realistically **half a day of clicking**, spread across two vendors, plus a
verification wait that is outside anyone's control. Start Meta verification the
same day the accounts are created.

### 13.4 The two constraints that shape the creative — read before writing ads

These are not compliance footnotes; they determine whether a campaign is
possible at all.

**1. Meta bans before/after and body-transformation imagery outright.** Every
piece of creative the current site would suggest is unrunnable. This is not a
grey area and it is not appealable.

**2. You cannot target people by health condition.** Meta treats health as a
sensitive category — there is no "people with incontinence" audience, and
attempting it gets the account flagged. So targeting is demographic and
interest-proxy only, and **the ad copy has to make the right person self-identify**.

That second constraint is the whole creative brief, and it is genuinely good
news: a line that a woman over 45 recognises herself in will outperform any
targeting parameter, and it is the one thing a competitor cannot copy from an
audience export. It also means the incontinence angle needs Stephanie's actual
words more than it needs a media budget.

### 13.5 What to spend first

Nothing, until §6.2's claims list exists. An ad that cannot state a benefit is a
$0 return regardless of budget, and a rejected ad account is slow to un-reject.

**Order:** accounts and verification (free, and the waiting starts) → organic via
Stephanie's network (free, already working) → a small paid test with a written
kill rule → scale only what the numbers earn.

### 13.6 Flexible payment — built 260728

David: *"we want as flexible a buying experience as possible."* Agreed, and it is
load-bearing for §3.4 — financing is what makes $599 a monthly decision rather
than a single one.

**What was actually wrong:** every PaymentIntent hardcoded
`payment_method_types: ['card']`, which silently excluded Klarna, Affirm, Link and
Cash App before a buyer ever saw the page. `PaymentElement` renders only what the
intent permits, so the client was never the blocker. Now
`automatic_payment_methods: { enabled: true }` — which methods appear is a
Dashboard setting, not a deploy.

**Live on the platform account (260728):** card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Link,
Cash App, **Klarna**, **Affirm**. Bancontact, EPS, giropay and iDEAL turned OFF —
European bank redirects, useless for a belt shipping to Florida.

| Method | Fee | On $599 |
|---|---|---|
| Card | 2.9% + 30¢ | $17.67 |
| **Klarna — Financing** | 2.99% + 30¢ | **$18.21** |
| Klarna — Pay in 4 | 5.99% + 30¢ | $36.20 |
| Affirm | 6% + 30¢ | $36.24 |

**Klarna Financing is card-priced.** Pay-in-4 and Affirm cost ~$18 more per unit —
paid only on sales that complete, a share of which would not have happened.

⚠️ **Klarna classifies medical devices as a RESTRICTED business.** There is no
separate Klarna application — Stripe holds the relationship, you toggle it, and

⚠️ **This is per Stripe account.** Direct charges use the CONNECTED account's
settings, so all of the above must be done again on David's. And the **Affirm
badge already on kessela.com is currently a promise checkout cannot keep.**

**The argument to use with David, because it is real:** Kenneth paid for a $129
car part with Klarna — three payments of ~$42 — with the cash sitting in his
account. He chose to keep the cash. That is not someone priced out at $599; it is
someone who would rather not spend it all today, and there are far more of those.

## 14. Where the incontinence proposition LIVES — one tenant, two front doors

Asked and answered 260728: do we redesign the Kessela site, or copy it?

**Neither.** `Tenants.domains` is an array — many domains resolve to one tenant,
one marked primary for canonical URLs. So this is an ADD.

**Kessela (tenant 30) keeps everything**: the product, the 2,500 units, orders,
the warranty queue, Stripe, the studies. What differs between the fat-loss and
incontinence propositions is **the entry point and the words** — that is pages,
not a portal.

**Why not a second tenant.** Inventory, orders, tickets and payments would
fragment across two of them. That is running two businesses to change a headline,
and the first sale through the "wrong" one means reconciling stock by hand.

**Why not a redesign.** It discards the fat-loss buyer, who does convert in
person, and it changes what David has already been shown — which was on his
open-questions list before any of this started.

### Staged, cheapest first

1. **Now — a landing page on the existing site.** Own slug, `showInNav: false`,
   because it is not a menu item, it is where Stephanie's link points. Own hero,
   own FAQ, own proof, same Add to Cart. Free, and it tests the message, which is
   exactly the "free swings first" promise in §3.4.
2. **If it converts — bind a second domain to the same tenant.** Something that
   says what it does: *Kessela Physique* is a body-contouring name and a woman
   searching for pelvic-floor help will not recognise herself in it. Same tenant,
   so inventory never splits. Keep `isPrimary` on the original so canonical URLs
   and SEO stay put.
3. **Only if it becomes a genuinely separate business** — own tenant, own brand,
   own stock. Months away. The platform does it natively when the time comes.

**Blocked on the claims list.** An incontinence landing page IS the claim; it is
not a design that happens to mention one. Structure can be built now as a draft
with the claims blank, so the day his list lands it is a paste, not a build.
