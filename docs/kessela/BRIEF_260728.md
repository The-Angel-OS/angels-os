## 2. The strategy — why this would work where it hasn't

**The problem was never the product, the ads, or the price. It was the aisle.**

Every previous attempt sold a **fat-loss belt**. That is the most crowded category
on the consumer internet, and the product cannot win there:

- A $599 belt sits beside a $99 one that looks identical in a search result
- The only imagery that would separate them — before-and-after — **is banned by
  Meta outright**, so the difference cannot be advertised
- Everybody overstates, so nobody's claims carry weight, honest ones included

No budget fixes being the expensive item in a commodity aisle.

**So we stop selling the crowded thing.** The 2,500 units were bought for
pelvic-floor reasons. In that market:

| | Fat loss | Pelvic floor |
|---|---|---|
| Compared against | a $99 belt | **physio at $100–200 a session** |
| $599 reads as | expensive | **cheap** |
| Urgency | discretionary | **now, and recurring** |
| Needs before/after | yes — and it's banned | **no** |
| Competition | saturated | **almost none** |

**Same product. Same price. Different aisle.**

## 3. Does $599 hold? Yes — and here is the mechanism

$599 already works **in person**, and it is worth being precise about why: **David
is the proof.** He hands someone the belt, they feel it, he tells them the story.
Three walk-ins bought three units at $500 cash without being pushed — that is a
demonstration closing, not price sensitivity.

Cold and online, the demonstration is missing. **So the job is to reproduce it at
a distance:**

1. **Stephanie, in her own words** — the story of why she believed in it
2. **The research stated straight**, including what each study does *not* show
3. **A spec table** — numbers where the page currently has adjectives
4. **Financing** — Klarna and Affirm, so $599 is a monthly figure

Each of those substitutes for something David does in the room. That is the test
for whether any piece of work belongs in this plan.

**And nothing in phase one costs money:** Stephanie's own network first, then
pelvic-floor, postpartum and menopause creators whose audiences *are* the buyer.
Paid advertising comes last, and only behind something that already worked for
free.

## 4. What I need from David

### 4.1 Sign off on the words — 30 seconds

Rather than ask for a list, I have written the sentences. **Initial or strike
each one** (full detail in `CLAIMS_SIGNOFF.md`):

- **B1** *"Electrical muscle stimulation causes muscles to contract. The Kessela
  Physique applies EMS across the lower abdomen and core, alongside red and
  near-infrared light."*
- **B2** *"Some people use it as part of a daily core routine. Others came to it
  for pelvic-floor reasons. We are not going to tell you which you are."*
- **B3** *"Stephanie believed in this enough that we brought in 2,500 units. She
  will tell you her story in her own words — what it did for her is hers to say,
  not ours to print."*
- **B4** *"A session with a pelvic-floor physiotherapist typically costs $100–200…
  The Kessela Physique is $599 once, at home, for as long as you own it."*
- **B5** *"We publish the research with its limits attached… We would rather be
  the one you can check."*
- **B6** *"Try it for 14 days. If it is not for you, send it back."*

**Why the care.** Kessela is a **registered** Class II device. Registration is a
listing with the FDA — it is not clearance and not approval. A cleared device may
state what it treats; a registered one may not. That single distinction decides
every line above, and the exposure sits on David's company. Being careful here is
not caution for its own sake — the pelvic-floor market is worth more than the
fat-loss one, which is exactly why it is worth building on something that holds.

**Three claims currently on kessela.com** — "clinically proven", "reduce fat",
"equal to 300 sit-ups" — are deliberately **not** on the new page. Bringing the
existing pages into line is a separate decision and David's to make.

### 4.2 A spec sheet

Wavelengths, irradiance, treatment area, LED count, EMS channels and frequency,
session length, certifications.

This is the direct answer to David's own point about comparing a Ferrari to a
tricycle. Nobody knows because the page has adjectives where it needs numbers,
and a spec table is the only thing that separates a $599 belt from a $99 one. It
is cheap to build once the numbers exist.

### 4.3 The call with Stephanie

45 minutes rather than 10. Right now the reason 2,500 units were bought lives in
one person's head, and it should be written down. What she is comfortable saying
publicly is entirely her decision.

### 4.4 One sentence on the 10%

Not a renegotiation — just so nobody remembers it differently later. Does it
cover site sales only, or in-person and cash too? Gross or net of processing?
Kessela only, or does it follow the books and the pet line?
