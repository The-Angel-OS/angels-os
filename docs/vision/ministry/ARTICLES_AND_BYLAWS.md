### ARTICLE IV — PURPOSE

The Corporation is organized exclusively for **religious, charitable, and educational** purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (or the corresponding section of any future federal tax code), including, but not limited to, the following:

(a) To advance the Christian ministry of bearing witness to the dignity of every person, with particular care for veterans, the elderly, the disabled, the housing-insecure, and others overlooked by the wider society, drawing on the Episcopal tradition of restorative justice and the corporal works of mercy;

(b) To establish, operate, and support **housing trusts** — including the acquisition, rehabilitation, holding, and stewardship of residential real property — for the long-term benefit of veterans, low-income tenants, and others displaced by predatory housing practices, and to provide pastoral, advocacy, and material support to such persons;

(c) To document, celebrate, and steward God's creation, including the marine, wildlife, and natural environments of Pinellas County, Florida, and the wider Tampa Bay region, through educational media, public engagement, citizen science, and direct service in cooperation with marine research institutions;

(d) To support veterans of all branches of the United States Armed Forces through chaplaincy, advocacy, fellowship, and material assistance, with particular attention to those navigating disability ratings, housing instability, reintegration, and pastoral need;

(e) To engage in such other charitable, religious, and educational activities as the Board of Directors may from time to time determine to be consistent with the foregoing purposes and with Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code.

### ARTICLE V — RESTRICTIONS ON ACTIVITIES

(a) **No private inurement.** No part of the net earnings of the Corporation shall inure to the benefit of, or be distributable to, its members, directors, officers, or other private persons, except that the Corporation shall be authorized and empowered to pay reasonable compensation for services rendered and to make payments and distributions in furtherance of the purposes set forth in Article IV.

(b) **No substantial lobbying.** No substantial part of the activities of the Corporation shall be the carrying on of propaganda, or otherwise attempting to influence legislation. The Corporation shall not participate in, or intervene in (including the publishing or distribution of statements), any political campaign on behalf of, or in opposition to, any candidate for public office.

(c) **501(c)(3) compliance.** Notwithstanding any other provision of these Articles, the Corporation shall not carry on any other activities not permitted to be carried on (i) by a corporation exempt from federal income tax under Section 501(c)(3), or (ii) by a corporation, contributions to which are deductible under Section 170(c)(2).

### ARTICLE VI — DISSOLUTION

Upon the dissolution of the Corporation, assets shall be distributed for one or more exempt purposes within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, or shall be distributed to the federal government, or to a state or local government, for a public purpose. Any such assets not so disposed of shall be disposed of by a Court of Competent Jurisdiction of the county in which the principal office of the Corporation is then located, exclusively for such purposes, or to such organization or organizations, as said Court shall determine, which are organized and operated exclusively for such purposes.

In the event of dissolution, **preference shall be given to distribution of assets to the Episcopal Diocese of Southwest Florida or its designated charitable arm**, provided that recipient is then a qualifying 501(c)(3) organization.

## Part 2 — Bylaws (Outline / Draft)

### ARTICLE I — NAME AND OFFICES

The name of the Corporation is Clearwater Cruisin' Ministries, Inc. (the "Corporation"). The principal office of the Corporation shall be located in Pinellas County, Florida, at such address as the Board of Directors shall determine.

### ARTICLE II — PURPOSE

The Corporation shall pursue the purposes set forth in its Articles of Incorporation.

### ARTICLE III — MEMBERS

The Corporation shall have no members. All corporate powers shall be exercised by or under the authority of the Board of Directors.

### ARTICLE IV — BOARD OF DIRECTORS

**Section 4.01 — Number.** The Board of Directors shall consist of not fewer than three (3) and not more than nine (9) Directors.

**Section 4.02 — Term.** Each Director shall serve a term of three (3) years, with terms staggered to ensure continuity. Initial terms shall be assigned by the founding Board to achieve staggered rotation.

**Section 4.03 — Qualifications.** Directors must be at least 18 years of age and must support the purposes of the Corporation. Directors are not required to be residents of Florida.

**Section 4.04 — Meetings.** The Board shall meet at least quarterly. Meetings may be held in person, by telephone, or by video conference. A majority of the Directors then in office shall constitute a quorum.

**Section 4.05 — Action Without a Meeting.** Any action required or permitted to be taken by the Board may be taken without a meeting if all Directors consent in writing (including electronically) to the action.

**Section 4.06 — Vacancies.** Vacancies on the Board shall be filled by a majority vote of the remaining Directors.

**Section 4.07 — Removal.** A Director may be removed, with or without cause, by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the Directors then in office.

### ARTICLE V — OFFICERS

**Section 5.01 — Officers.** The Corporation shall have a President, a Secretary, and a Treasurer, each elected by the Board of Directors. The same person may hold the offices of Secretary and Treasurer.

**Section 5.02 — President.** The President shall preside at all meetings of the Board, supervise the affairs of the Corporation, and perform such other duties as the Board may direct.

**Section 5.03 — Secretary.** The Secretary shall keep the minutes of all Board meetings, give notice of meetings, and maintain the corporate records.

**Section 5.04 — Treasurer.** The Treasurer shall have custody of the funds of the Corporation, keep accurate financial records, and present financial reports at each Board meeting and annually to the full Board.

### ARTICLE VI — CONFLICT OF INTEREST POLICY

**Section 6.01 — Purpose.** The Corporation shall maintain a written Conflict of Interest Policy applicable to all Directors and Officers, designed to protect the Corporation's interest when it is contemplating entering into a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of an Officer, Director, or other interested person.

**Section 6.02 — Required Disclosure.** Any Director or Officer who has a direct or indirect financial interest in any transaction under consideration shall disclose that interest to the Board and shall not participate in the deliberation or vote on the matter.

**Section 6.03 — Annual Statement.** Each Director and Officer shall sign an annual statement affirming that he or she has received, read, and understands the Conflict of Interest Policy and has complied with it during the preceding year.

### ARTICLE VII — INDEMNIFICATION

The Corporation shall indemnify its Directors, Officers, employees, and agents to the fullest extent permitted by Chapter 617 of the Florida Statutes.

### ARTICLE VIII — FISCAL YEAR

The fiscal year of the Corporation shall be the calendar year, unless otherwise determined by the Board.

### ARTICLE IX — AMENDMENT

These Bylaws may be amended by a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the Directors then in office, provided notice of the proposed amendment is given to all Directors at least fourteen (14) days in advance of the vote.

### ARTICLE X — DISSOLUTION

Upon dissolution, the Corporation's assets shall be distributed in accordance with Article VI of the Articles of Incorporation.
