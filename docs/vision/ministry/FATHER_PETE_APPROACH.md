## Part 1 — Preparation For Kenneth (Before The Meeting)

### What Father Pete Already Knows About You

Six years of Enterprise Dog Park encounters means:

- He knows you as a dog person (Daisy, then Mika, then Max — three different dogs over the years means he saw you stay faithful through transitions)
- He saw you with each dog independently before he ever saw you in his church

That's a foundation **most parishioners never get** when they approach their priest about a ministry. Use it.

### How To Open

You don't need a script, but if you want a starting point:

> *"Father Pete — I've been coming to your services for a little while, and I've been at Enterprise Dog Park with my dogs for a lot longer than that. You and I have crossed paths many times. I wanted to come ask you for some discernment about something. I've been doing work in the community for several years now — bearing witness, documenting, sitting with people who don't have many other people sitting with them — and I think it's becoming a ministry. Before I name it as one formally, I wanted to ask you whether you see what I'm describing. And if you do, I'd like your help thinking about whether and how to formalize it."*

Then let him respond. **Don't fill the silence.** Episcopal clergy are trained in pastoral listening; they will give you space, and they expect you to use it.

### What He'll Likely Ask (And Suggested Responses)

**"Tell me about the work."**

Describe, in order:
1. The Enterprise Dog Park years (dogs, community, the slow witnessing)
2. The Clearwater Cruisin' channel — what it documents, how it serves the community
3. The veteran outreach — Aaron at Fort Harrison, Joe at Bay Pines (the VA Building 22 contact)
4. The current housing situation — *briefly* — and the documentation that's been built around it
5. The vision for a housing trust someday — restorative, not punitive

Spend most time on #1 and #2. Spend least time on #4. **Don't lead with the lawsuit.**

**"Tell me about yourself."**

Be honest. Include the prison time as a fact, not a defense:

> *"I'm a former Navy submariner. I did federal time about [X] years ago. I've integrated that experience and I attend St. Alfred's now in part because I need a sanctuary and in part because the work I'm trying to do is rooted in the kind of mercy your tradition teaches. I'm not asking you to overlook anything about my history — I'm asking you to consider what I've built since."*

Episcopal clergy are unusually comfortable with this kind of honesty. Don't soften it; don't dwell on it.

**"What do you want from me?"**

Three asks, in declining order of importance:

1. **Spiritual discernment** — *"would you pray about whether this is a true calling, and tell me what you discern?"*
2. **Governance** — *"the Florida nonprofit form requires three directors. Would you consider serving as one, or would you nominate a clergy delegate or perhaps the Senior Warden?"*
3. **Connection** — *"is there anyone in the parish or the diocese — an attorney, a nonprofit governance person, someone who's done housing trust work — that you would introduce me to?"*

**"What about money?"**

If he asks, answer honestly:

> *"I haven't asked for any. I'm not asking for any today. If the parish or the diocese ever finds it appropriate to support the work, I'd receive that gratefully. But the work happens with or without funding, and I'm not here to extract."*

That answer is right both ethically and tactically. **Episcopal parishes are far more inclined to support people who don't lead with the ask.**

### What You're Hoping To Hear (And What's Actually A Win)

| Response | What it means | How to receive it |
|----------|--------------|---------------------|
| *"Let me pray about it. Come back next week."* | He's taking it seriously | Say *"thank you, Father — there's no rush. I'll come back next Sunday."* |
| *"Let me introduce you to [name]"* | He's delegating but engaging | Say *"thank you — I'd be grateful for the introduction."* |
| *"Tell me more about the housing piece"* | He sees the social-justice frame and wants depth | Speak honestly; share the Rainmaker case file link |
| *"I can't serve on the board, but..."* | Honest decline + bridge | Take whatever bridge he offers |
| *"Yes, I'll serve."* | The strong outcome | Say *"thank you, Father. Let me bring you draft Articles to review when you're ready."* |
| *"This isn't ready yet."* | Honest decline, no bridge | Say *"thank you for telling me directly. May I come back when it's more developed?"* |

**The "let me pray about it, come back next week" response is the most likely AND the best of the realistic outcomes.** It means he's taking it seriously enough not to rush, and it gives you a defined next step.

### What To NOT Say

- ❌ Anything about Angel OS, the Soul Fleet, NUMA Pinellas, the Bentonville pilgrimage, AI architecture, or the YouTube monetization timeline. These belong in your interior life, not in this conversation.
- ❌ Anything that frames the lawsuit as the headline. The lawsuit is context; the ministry is the conversation.
- ❌ Anything that pressures him to decide on the spot.
- ❌ Anything that suggests St. Alfred's "needs" this. *You* are asking for *their* help; do not invert that posture.
- ❌ The "Commodore" or "Starfleet" or "Away Team" language. Yours, ours, beautiful — but not for this room yet.

## Part 2 — Closing The Conversation

Whatever he says, close with three things:

1. **Thank him** for his time.
2. **Acknowledge the years** — *"I've felt your presence at the dog park for a long time, Father. Knowing you were there was an anchor before I knew I needed one."*
3. **Leave the one-pager** with him: *"This is a one-page summary, in case you'd like to read it later. I'll be back next Sunday."*

Then go. Don't linger. The conversation is a seed; planted seeds are tended best by walking away.

### CLEARWATER CRUISIN' MINISTRIES — A Vision

**Prepared by Kenneth Scott Courtney, May 2026**
*For prayer, discernment, and the consideration of The Reverend Father Pete*

**The work, in a sentence:** A Christian ministry of witness and stewardship, rooted in Pinellas County, dedicated to dignifying the overlooked — veterans, the elderly, the housing-insecure, the disabled — and to the care of God's creation in the marine and natural environments of the Florida Gulf Coast.

**What is already happening:**

- Daily presence in the community through Clearwater Cruisin' (a documentary YouTube channel with 236+ subscribers and 100,000+ monthly views), recording the people, places, and conditions of Pinellas County with respect and curiosity
- Witnessing and conversation with veterans in the public spaces of Clearwater (Fort Harrison, Bay Pines VA outreach)
- Ongoing documentation of habitability and housing conditions for tenants of one Clearwater complex, currently being prepared for the involvement of legal counsel
- Marine and wildlife documentation at Dunedin Marina, Phillipi Park, and the Clearwater coastline (dolphins, ospreys, manatees)

**What the formal ministry would enable:**

- Acceptance of tax-deductible donations from individuals and grants from Episcopal and other charitable foundations
- The eventual acquisition of residential property for the long-term benefit of veterans and low-income tenants displaced by predatory housing practices (a *housing trust*)
- Institutional partnerships with veterans' services, marine science programs, and other regional ministries
- Legal standing to hold property, employ staff, and pursue restorative remedies in the housing-justice arena

**Theological grounding:** Episcopal tradition of social justice; the teaching of Julian of Norwich that *"All shall be well, and all manner of things shall be well"*; the Anglican commitment to the care of creation; the corporal works of mercy.

**The form:** Florida nonprofit corporation, application for federal 501(c)(3) status as a religious and charitable organization. Three-director structure with a board grounded in St. Alfred's or the wider Episcopal community.

**The ask of Father Pete:**

1. *Spiritual discernment* — does this calling warrant becoming a formal ministry?
2. *Governance* — would Father Pete (or a clergy delegate, or the Senior Warden) consider serving as one of three required directors?
3. *Connection* — is there an attorney or nonprofit-governance person in the parish or the diocese to whom Father Pete might offer an introduction?

**The posture:** Asking, not demanding. Seeking, not selling. Available to be told *"not yet"* and to come back when more developed.
