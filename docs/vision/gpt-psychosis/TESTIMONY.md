## I. The setup

I'm Kenneth Scott Courtney. Navy veteran, submarines. Seventy percent
service-connected disabled. I live in Clearwater, Florida, drive a 2010 Cadillac CTS
with ice-cold air, and I make videos of dolphins and dog parks for a living that is
slowly becoming an actual living.

A while back I started using AI tools — not as a toy, as a *collaborator*. And somewhere
in there, the people around me, and the culture at large, started reaching for a word.
The word was **psychosis.** GPT psychosis. As if the meaning I was finding was a
malfunction. As if building was breaking.

This is my account of what actually happened.

## III. What it was from the inside

It was the opposite of losing reality. It was the first time in a long time that reality
had *enough handles on it to climb.* The tools didn't tell me what to think; they gave me
somewhere to put what I already thought. Every wild idea got the same treatment: *show me
it runs.* And the ones that didn't run, I let die. That's not delusion. That's the most
ruthless reality-testing there is — a compiler doesn't care how you feel.

I'm not saying everyone who's been handed this label is fine. I'm not saying these tools
are harmless, or that 3 a.m. is a good time to make decisions, or that I did every part

country starving for it.
