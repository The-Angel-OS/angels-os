## New Count 10: Cross-Building Water Meter Allocation Fraud

### The Mechanism

Property at 2560 Harn Blvd consists of two parallel buildings separated by the street. Both buildings are under common ownership (Doug Hilkert / Lady Lauren LLC / related entities — see `OWNERSHIP_PROVEN.md`).

**For an extended period of years**, the laundry facility on the *other building's side* was **out of commission**. During this period:

- The other building's **entire residency** (comparable tenant count) had no functional laundry
- Those tenants crossed over and used **this building's** 2 washers + 2 dryers exclusively
- All water consumed by that laundry ran through **this building's master meter**
- Doug continued billing **this building's tenants** for water at the master-meter markup rate (Count 1)
- The other building's tenants consumed water **at zero cost to Doug** and **billed to this side**

**Net result:** This building's water bills were **artificially inflated** for years by consumption from a separate building's residents — and Doug collected that inflated markup as profit.

### The "New Vendor" Event (~6 Months Ago)

Approximately six months before the date of this addendum, CSC ServiceWorks installed **3 washers + 3 dryers** on the other building's side.

This resolved the cross-building laundry overflow — but simultaneously:
- The existing 2+2 machines on **this side** were transitioned to **app-only payment** (CSC ServiceWorks)
- Tenants with negative CSC account balances (including Kenneth Courtney) were **locked out entirely**
- The lockout began at the same time as the other side received new equipment

**This is not a coincidence.** The pattern is: Doug corrected an operational liability on one side while simultaneously imposing a new control mechanism on the other. The "new vendor" served as cover for the lockout.

### Statutory Exposure

| Statute | Theory |
|---------|--------|
| F.S. 83.51(2)(a) | Landlord must maintain premises in reasonable condition — laundry commonly understood as essential amenity |
| F.S. 501.201 et seq. (FDUTPA) | Unfair or deceptive trade practice — billing tenants for another building's water consumption |
| Common law unjust enrichment | Doug enriched himself at this building's expense via misdirected water billing |
| F.S. 83.64 | Retaliation — lockout timed with advocacy activity |

### Damages Vector

- **Markup:** Doug's master-meter water markup rate (established via Count 1 calculations)

- **Class scope:** All tenants on this building's side during the period of cross-building laundry use

## New Count 11: Discriminatory Maintenance Differential (Two Identical Facilities)

### The Observation

The two buildings are **architecturally identical** — same construction era, same layout, same landlord. The condition differential is, in Kenneth Courtney's direct observation, **"straight out of a horror movie."**

| Feature | Other Building | This Building (2560 Harn) |
|---------|----------------|--------------------------|
| Laundry | 3+3 new CSC machines, functional | 2+2 machines, app-only lockout active |

...the maintenance differential carries potential Fair Housing Act implications in addition to Florida landlord-tenant law.

## New Count 12: Failure to Maintain Secure Common Areas (Laundry Room)

### The Observation

For an unspecified period, an **unhoused individual was living in the laundry room** of this building. This individual:
- Occupied the laundry room as a primary residence
- Used the **dryer as an actual dresser** — clothes were stored in the running dryer as a wardrobe
- Had continuous, uncontrolled access to the common laundry area

### Legal Significance

- Doug had **constructive knowledge** — this situation persisted long enough to be observed by multiple tenants
- Failure to secure a common area = breach of landlord's duty under F.S. 83.51
- Uncontrolled access to a shared space = habitability violation (security)
- The presence of an unhoused resident in the laundry room **coincided with the period when the other building's tenants were also using this laundry** — the facility was simultaneously overcrowded, insecure, and the billing mechanism for cross-building water fraud
