## Count 6 — Pre-Code Knob-and-Tube Era Electrical Service Infrastructure

### Evidence

A wooden service panel mounted on bare wood backing serves as the main electrical distribution for at least one section of the property. The panel features:

- **Vertical ceramic cartridge fuses** (1940s-1950s vintage)
- **Cloth-clad insulated conductors** (pre-modern NEC, frequently asbestos-clad)
- **Individual paper circuit tags** hand-labeled
- **Duke Energy / Florida Power utility tag** reading: "FIXED COUNT DO NOT OPEN"

In-unit subpanels are 1960s-1970s vintage:

- **GE Load Center, Cat. TLM1212S Mod 1 / TLM1212F Mod 1** (125-amp service, 24 poles max)
- **ITE Circuit Breaker Company panels** (Industrial Test Equipment / pre-Siemens-acquisition vintage, Atlanta GA manufactured)
- **Hand-scrawled cardboard circuit directories** taped inside doors (NEC 408.4(A) violation — requires *permanent* legible legend)
- **Wall cracks** running vertically from above the panel toward the service entry (structural settling near live electrical)

### Why This Matters

1. **Knob-and-tube wiring is uninsurable in Florida** — most landlord/homeowner insurance policies refuse to write or maintain coverage on knob-and-tube installations. If Doug carries insurance on the property, it was almost certainly obtained without disclosing the panel type to the insurer = **insurance fraud (FL Statute 817.234)**.

2. **Cloth-clad wiring is frequently asbestos-clad** in pre-1970s installations = **environmental hazard, EPA-regulated**, potential personal injury exposure.

3. **No GFCI/AFCI protection** — these systems predate ground-fault interrupter technology by 40+ years. Directly explains the Kenmore stove electrical shock hazard documented in v2.3 Count 2.

4. **ITE panels are in the same documented fire-hazard category as Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels** — known for breakers failing to trip during overcurrent events.

5. **GE TLM1212-series replacement breakers are obsolete** — any breaker replaced in the past decade was either mismatched/unrated or sourced through specialty channels.

### Statutory Citations

- **F.S. 83.51(1)(a)** — landlord obligation to maintain electrical systems in safe condition
- **Florida Building Code (FBC) – Existing Building Code** — requires upgrade to current NEC standards when renovation triggers code application
- **NEC 408.4(A)** — circuit directory must be permanent and legible
- **F.S. 817.234** — insurance fraud, if landlord insurance application omitted disclosure of panel type

## Count 7 — Forced Conversion of Coin-Op to App-Op Laundry: Active Service Denial

### Evidence

The communal laundry facility has been converted from coin-operated to **CSC ServiceWorks app/card-only payment**:

- Older Whirlpool top-load units retain physically present but **de-activated coin slots** ($2.00 mechanisms visible but non-functional)
- Newer Speed Queen units are **app-only with no coin acceptors at all** ("Pay with Your Phone" QR codes, "First Wash Free with the App")
- Tenant CSC ServiceWorks account balance is currently **negative**, locking tenant out of service that water bill (paid via master meter) is currently funding

### Why This Matters

This is theft of services with a clean, current, ongoing denial:

1. Original install: coin-operated communal laundry tied to master water meter (tenants pay water via rent/utility allocation)
2. Conversion: Doug switched to CSC ServiceWorks app/card system, presumably entering a revenue-share or net-payment arrangement that flows revenue directly to Doug or affiliated entity rather than via coin-box collection
3. **Tenants who don't load funds onto the app are locked out** of laundry service while continuing to pay for the water that runs the machines

The dollar amount per tenant is modest but the **legal posture is severe**: this is not a 9-year-old historical complaint — it is happening *right now, this week*, with negative balances on file and physical coins in pockets that cannot be used. **This grounds a TRO motion if eviction proceedings begin.**

### Statutory Citations

- **F.S. 83.51(2)(a)(2)** — landlord must provide running water (extends to facilities tenant pays for)
- **F.S. 772.11** — civil theft, treble damages
- **F.S. 501.204** — FDUTPA (deceptive practice — collecting water payment while denying water-paid service)

## Count 8 — Master-Meter Diversion to Unused Irrigation System

## Count 9 — Property-Wide Decommissioned Roof/Wall Vents = Pest Ingress Vector

### Evidence

Aerial / exterior photography of the building roofline reveals:

- **Multiple roof exhaust vent positions visible** as "missing teeth" — locations where exhaust caps should be but have been removed
- **Pattern is systemic across the property**, not isolated to one unit
- **Corresponds directly to the drywalled-over in-unit laundry hookups** (v2.3 Count 5) and the open wall cavity vents (interior photos, May 21, 2026)

### Why This Matters

This evidence transforms v2.3 Count 5 from a single-unit habitability failure into a **property-wide structural defect that Doug engineered**:

1. Drywalled over the in-unit vent openings (Unit 13 documented)
2. Removed the corresponding roof exhaust caps (aerial photos)
3. Left in-wall ductwork as **un-sealed pest highways through the building envelope**

The bed bugs, cockroaches, and ants have been traveling between units through these un-capped HVAC routes for 9+ years. This is the structural mechanism that explains why the 3-year bed bug infestation in Unit 13 could not be eradicated by tenant-level remediation — the building itself is the vector.

### Statutory Citations

- **F.S. 83.51(1)(b)** — landlord obligation to maintain extermination of rats, mice, roaches, ants, wood-destroying organisms, and bedbugs (with statutory exceptions not applicable here)
- **FBC Mechanical Code** — exhaust ventilation requirements
- **F.S. 501.204** — FDUTPA (concealed structural defect across class)
