## III. COUNTS

### COUNT I — Breach of Implied Warranty of Habitability (F.S. 83.51)

Florida Statute 83.51 requires landlords to maintain premises in compliance with applicable building, housing, and health codes, and to maintain in good working order: plumbing, heating/cooling, hot water, and electrical systems.

Douglas Hilkert knowingly and systematically failed to:

1. **Replace a 30-year-old HVAC system** that had degraded far below functional efficiency, driving tenant electric bills from \~$89/month to $340-$360/month and ultimately stabilizing around \~$280/month — still representing a 200%+ overcharge sustained for the duration of each tenancy.  
2. **Remediate plastered-over 240V electrical endpoints** that created active shock hazards in residential units.  
3. **Remediate plastered-over water access points** that created flood risk and impeded maintenance.  
4. **Replace or repair a Kenmore stove** that delivered electrical shocks to tenants during normal cooking use.  
5. **Eradicate a bedbug infestation** that persisted for approximately **three years** in Mr. Courtney's unit, causing documented sleep disruption, anxiety, PTSD symptoms, and property damage.

As a licensed real-estate attorney, Hilkert cannot claim ignorance of these obligations. His failure was knowing and willful.

### COUNT II — Violation of Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (F.S. 501.204, FDUTPA)

The landlord's knowing retention of a 30-year-old HVAC system — without disclosure to tenants that the system's degraded efficiency would inflate their utility costs by 200-300% — constitutes an unfair and deceptive trade practice. Tenants paid market rent with the reasonable expectation of functional climate control; they instead received a system that operated as a concealed surcharge of approximately $180/month.

This is utility fraud by omission. FDUTPA provides for **treble damages** under F.S. 501.211(2) and mandatory attorney fees under F.S. 501.2105.

**Per-tenant utility fraud (Mr. Courtney):**

* Average monthly overcharge: \~$180  
* Duration: 108 months  
* Base utility fraud: **$19,440**  
* Treble damages: **$58,320**

### COUNT III — Retaliatory Conduct (F.S. 83.64)

Following Mr. Courtney's complaints regarding habitability conditions, Douglas Hilkert sent a retaliatory communication at **5:46 PM** — a text message that constituted intimidation and retaliation against the exercise of tenant rights.

F.S. 83.64 prohibits landlord retaliation against tenants who complain about code violations or exercise legal rights. The timing, tone, and context of the communication establish retaliatory intent. The tenant is entitled to actual damages, costs, and attorney fees.

### COUNT IV — Fraudulent/Deceptive Corporate Structure (Alter Ego / Veil Piercing)

Douglas Hilkert, a licensed attorney, structured his ownership of \~22 residential units across two buildings through multiple shell LLCs and a family nominee (his sister) to:

1. Obscure his personal beneficial ownership from tenants, courts, and taxing authorities  
2. Insulate himself from habitability and fraud claims  
3. Potentially manipulate tax obligations through related-party transactions and artificial entity separation

Under Florida's alter ego doctrine, the corporate veil should be pierced where:

* The LLC is a "mere instrumentality" of the individual (Hilkert controls all management, maintenance, and financial decisions across all entities)  
* There is commingling or unity of interest between the individual and entities  
* The corporate form is used to perpetrate fraud, injustice, or an inequitable result

**Discovery will seek:** All LLC formation documents, operating agreements, tax returns, property appraiser records, bank statements, and inter-entity transfers for all Hilkert-affiliated entities holding title to units at 2566 and 2560 Harn Blvd.

### COUNT V — HOA Monopoly / Laundry Surcharge

The properties operate under an HOA structure that Hilkert controls by virtue of owning 22 of 24 units. This HOA imposes mandatory laundry facility fees of approximately $40/month per unit with:

* No tenant representation in HOA governance  
* No competitive bidding for laundry services  
* No opt-out provision  
* Hilkert as de facto sole decision-maker by unit-count majority

This constitutes an unfair trade practice and a self-dealing extraction mechanism. Over 108 months: **$4,320 per tenant**.
