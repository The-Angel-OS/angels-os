### The Statutory Math

| Date | Event | Statute |
|------|-------|---------|
| May 1, 2026 | Written habitability complaint | F.S. 83.64(1)(c) — protected activity |
| May 27, 2026 | Notice of Termination served | F.S. 83.64(1) — adverse action |

Under F.S. 83.64, where a landlord terminates a tenancy within 6 months of a tenant's written good-faith habitability complaint, **a rebuttable presumption of retaliation arises**. The burden then falls on the landlord to prove the termination is not retaliatory.

**Doug's elevated burden as a Florida-licensed real-estate attorney** (Florida Bar member): he is charged with constructive knowledge of F.S. 83.64. He cannot claim ignorance of the statute. His "good faith" defense is preemptively foreclosed by his bar credentials.

## New Count 15: Fraudulent Inducement of Tenancy

### The Acquisition-Stripping Pattern

Per Kenneth's account, Doug acquired Unit 13 (through Luke Alexander LLC) **around the time of Kenneth's release from prison** (approximately 2020-2021). The in-unit washer/dryer hookups were **already stripped and covered** when Kenneth signed the lease.

**This is not removal-during-tenancy.** This is **acquisition → strip → lease in degraded condition** without disclosure.

A reasonable tenant signing a lease at typical Pinellas market rates would assume:
- The unit was built and maintained according to its original spec
- All amenities present at construction remain available unless explicitly disclosed otherwise
- The landlord has not affirmatively stripped capability from the unit between prior tenancy and current tenancy

### The Heightened Duty (Johnson v. Davis)

Under Florida common law, a seller of residential property must disclose any fact materially affecting the value of the property that is not readily observable and is not known to the buyer. *Johnson v. Davis*, 480 So.2d 625 (Fla. 1985). The same heightened duty has been extended to landlords in residential leasing contexts.

**Doug Hilkert is a Florida-licensed real-estate attorney.** His professional knowledge of disclosure obligations is established as a matter of law. Concealment of a stripped amenity from an incoming tenant — particularly a disabled veteran with limited bargaining power — constitutes:

- **Fraudulent inducement** (common law, *Johnson v. Davis*)
- **FDUTPA violation** (deceptive trade practice)
- **Possible Bar discipline** under Rule 4-8.4(c) (dishonesty in business dealings)

### The Pattern Across the Class

If this acquisition-strip-lease pattern holds across Doug's and Cynthia's combined 20-unit portfolio (per the Colliers/Crexi listing), the class is every tenant who signed a lease for a unit that had in-unit hookups *at the time of family acquisition* but not at the time of the tenant's signing.
