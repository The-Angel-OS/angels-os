We camped the night before, woke up to that crisp mountain air, and signed up for a High Adventure 4‑person raft — just the three of us and a guide.

The Upper Gauley delivered exactly what it’s famous for:

• Iron Ring — huge, technical, and lined with videoboaters on the rocks
• Sweet’s Falls — the iconic 14‑foot drop
• Fluffy Box of Kittens — the Class III that flips more boats than it should
• Woods Ferry — the final Class IV before the takeout
• And of course Pillow Rock, which we hit hard (though some of that footage didn’t survive the years)

The Gauley is one of the top whitewater runs in North America, fed by controlled releases from Summersville Dam that turn the river into a roller coaster every fall. Songer Whitewater was one of the premier outfitters of that era — known for their guides, their culture, and their no‑nonsense river expertise. They later became part of what is now Adventures on the Gorge, but back then, Songer had its own soul.

**Pure 1998 nostalgia.**

This is the **original Songer Whitewater** professional river tour video that was sold to rafters at the takeout for about $50 back in the late 90s/early 2000s. If you rafted the Gauley with Songer during that era, this footage will hit you like a Sweet’s Falls boof — instant recognition of the videoboater narration, the classic soundtrack, the big-water hits, and that unmistakable river guide energy.

In this trip (circa 1998), my brother, his engineer friend, and I signed up for a High Adventure 4-person raft with a Songer guide. We camped the night before near their base camp off US-19 by the New River Gorge Bridge, woke up to crisp West Virginia mountain air, and took on one of the best big-water runs in North America.

**Iconic Rapids Featured:**
- **Iron Ring** — huge, technical, lined with videoboaters on the rocks
- **Sweet’s Falls** — the legendary 14-foot drop
- **Fluffy Box of Kittens** — the Class III that flips more boats than it should
- **Woods Ferry** — final Class IV before takeout
- **Pillow Rock** — we hit it hard (some of that footage sadly didn’t survive the years)

The Upper Gauley, fed by controlled releases from Summersville Dam, turns into a legitimate roller coaster every fall. Songer Whitewater was *the* outfitter back then — known for skilled guides, strong culture, and no-nonsense river expertise (they later became part of Adventures on the Gorge).

This video perfectly captures the muddy surge after the Meadow River confluence, the guide commentary, the swims, the chaos, the laughter, and the pure adrenaline that made a Gauley weekend unforgettable.

If you ever ran the river with Songer, this will take you straight back.
